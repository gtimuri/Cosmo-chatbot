Project name: Soft magnetic robots

What problem does this innovation aim to solve?
 Example 1: Alzheimer’s disease 
In the treatment of Alzheimer’s, fine electrodes are sometimes inserted into the brain to 
stimulate certain areas. How ever, the procedure is complex and often stressful for patients. 
Example 2: Artificial fertilization
 In artificial fertilization, specialists must select individual sperm cells. This requires a lot of 
skill, as the rigid tools used can easily damage the fragile cells. 

What changes does this innovation bring? 
The soft magnetic robots are just a few millimetres in size, soft and flexible. They need no 
electricity or batteries. This allows them to be used inside the body without damaging tissue.
 Example 1: Alzheimer’s disease 
The robot could be inserted into the brain through a very small opening. It stimulates the brain 
externally using a helmet – no wires required. 
Example 2: Artificial fertilization 
The robot can collect individual cells such as sperm without damaging them. 

Who will benefit from this innovation? 
• Patients 
• Medical professionals 

What is the technology behind it and how does it work? 
The robot is made of lightweight, soft rubber that contains magnetic material.
 The robot itself is a magnet. It moves with the help of an external magnetic field – no cables 
needed. It also contains sensors that continuously adapt the robot's movement. 

Challenges 
• A lengthy approval process for use as a medical device is necessary. 
• Robot must be biocompatible, in other words, it must be safe for the body. • Robot must work 
reliably over long periods. 
• Technology should use minimal energy to operate without a battery.

 When will this innovation be available?
Approximately 2035 to 2040
