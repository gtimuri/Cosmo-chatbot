Project name: Tactile capabilities for robots

What problem does this innovation aim to solve?
Robots do not (yet) have the sensitivity that humans have with their hands. Currently, they can
only grasp. That is why they are not yet able to perform tasks that require close proximity - for 
example in care. In future, robots with the ability to touch should be able to establish trust and 
create proximity.

What changes does this innovation bring? 
Robots are no longer just functional. They will become real interacting partners that can inspire 
trust in people.

Who will benefit from this innovation? 
Primarily people in care and medicine. Robots can support senior citizens at home and relieve 
the burden on care staff.

What is the technology behind it and how does it work? 
Neuroscience and engineering researchers are investigating how touch creates trust and how 
robots can perform it similar to humans. Therefore, they are developing small, sensitive sensors 
and artificial skin. 
The new robot technology should also be able to interpret facial expressions, body language 
and vital signs such as heartbeat or temperature. In this way, robots should be able to 
understand the meaning of touch even better in the future.

Challenges 
  Development of sensitive sensors: Robots must have sensors that can perceive touch as 
accurately as human skin. These sensors must not be larger than fingers. 
  Understanding of touch: Robots must learn to interpret touch correctly to enable meaningful 
interactions. 
  Building trust: Robots need to build trust through their touch, which is one of the biggest 
challenges in human-robot interaction.

 When will this innovation be available?
Approximately 2040 to 2045
